title: Runtime
description: How an always-on agent actually runs — a persistent event-sourced loop, goal missions that never finish on their own, mission constraints as the stop button, and no human approval mid-run.
---

This page answers the question a reviewer really has: *once I start this thing,
what stops it?*

The answer is not an approval queue. It is a set of limits you declare before it
starts, and this page states them, including the one that surprises people.

## The runtime is a loop, not a job runner

The engine ticks on a short fixed interval over a live world model. Every change
is an event appended to a timeline, with a periodic snapshot-and-trim so the
replay cost after a restart stays bounded rather than growing with the mission's
age.

**What that gets you:** a mission that runs for weeks is the shape this is built
for, not a batch job left running. A restart resumes from a snapshot instead of
replaying everything since the beginning.

## A goal mission never finishes on its own

Missions come in two shapes, and the difference is the whole always-on story:

| Mission | How it ends |
|---|---|
| **No goal** | Mechanically, at quiescence — when nothing is running and no remaining work can be dispatched. |
| **Has a goal** | Only when the Decider ends it. |

The completion system explicitly skips missions that have a goal. So a goal like
*"watch for newly published CVEs affecting services in the graph"* simply keeps
running. That is by design, not a leak.

The loop is event-driven, not polled. When new evidence lands and no decision is
already in flight, the Decider is asked what to do next; it reads the mission's
own slice of the world plus the catalog of components it may use, and
dispatches more work. The results become new evidence, and round it goes.

## Gibson owns no clock

There is no cron in the platform, and no trigger field on a mission. **A mission
starts because something outside Gibson started it** — your CI, a webhook, your
alert manager, your own scheduler.

This is deliberate and worth being plain about: an "always-on agent" here means a
mission that stays alive and reacts to evidence, not a scheduler that wakes
things up. If you want a nightly sweep, you own the nightly trigger.

## No human approves an action mid-run

Once a mission starts inside its declared bounds, it runs to completion with no
human checkpoint. There is no approval step and no escalation step. The runtime
removed them, deliberately, and recorded the reasoning as an ADR.

The argument is that runtime gates contradict an autonomous decider, and that
safety is better expressed as bounds declared up front than as a prompt somebody
clicks through at 2am. The consequence is stated in the ADR without softening:
**a mission launched inside its Rules of Engagement can run to completion with no
human checkpoint, and safety rests entirely on those bounds, the grants, and the
budget being correct.**

Say that out loud in your review. A reviewer who assumes there is an approval
step and discovers otherwise later is the outcome this page exists to prevent.

What a human still controls, at any time: the grants (see
[Authorization](/docs/security/authorization)), whether the component is enabled
at all, and the constraints below.

## Mission constraints are the stop button

Constraints are carried by the mission definition itself, so a published mission
is self-describing rather than depending on whoever launches it to supply limits.
**Zero means unlimited** for every numeric field.

| Constraint | Bounds |
|---|---|
| `max_duration` | Wall-clock for the whole mission |
| `max_cost` | Cumulative model spend, in USD |
| `max_tokens` | Cumulative tokens across every agent |
| `max_tokens_per_call` | Ceiling on any single model call |
| `max_turns_per_agent` | Observe/think/act iterations for one agent |
| `max_findings` | Stop after this many findings |
| `blocked_tools` | Tools that must not be invoked |
| `blocked_domains` | Network domains agents must not contact |
| `allowed_techniques` / `blocked_techniques` | Allow-list and block-list of technique IDs |
| `severity_threshold` | Minimum severity required to record a finding |
| `require_evidence` | Findings must carry proof |

An always-on mission is therefore a deliberate configuration: leave
`max_duration` at zero and put a ceiling on cost instead. The budget is also the
runaway guard on the decider itself.

Constraints are enforced by the daemon, not by the agent. `blocked_tools` is
applied at dispatch. The budget accumulates on every model call and stops the
mission when it is exceeded.

## What to check

1. A mission with a goal does not complete mechanically.
2. Nothing in the platform starts a mission on a timer.
3. There is no approval or escalation step at runtime.
4. Constraints live on the mission definition, and zero means unlimited.
5. `blocked_tools` is enforced at dispatch by the daemon, not by the agent.
